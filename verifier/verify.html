What this proves and what it doesn't

This ledger is a commitment scheme with delayed opening. A sealed draw is a real commitment even though you cannot read the number yet: the tail published at session zero commits to the entire column, so a public record naming (slot, lane, position) pins that value. Only the GM can compute it, and even the GM cannot change it, since any preimage published later must hash forward to a tail fixed before play began.

Sealed values are withheld because the game requires secret checks. Pathfinder's secret-check rule exists so a player doesn't learn their Recall Knowledge was a 3 and conclude the answer was unreliable. The withholding protects the player.

What it proves live: fair generation, if the two ceremony messages were witnessed in order: first the secret commitment, then the configuration commitment, then player nonces, with at least one nonce generated on a player-controlled device after the configuration was fixed. The verifier recomputes both commitments, but a ledger cannot prove when an external chat message was posted or how a nonce was generated. Given that ceremony, neither side can steer values. It also proves custody and count (every draw names slot, lane, position, session, and check type, with positions running 1, 2, 3 and no gaps), an append-only hash-chained history, timing on a stated publish cadence, open-lane values at each publish, and ordered slot allocation, so the GM cannot shop among pre-read columns.

Keep your own nonce and the genesis head. The configuration commitment is posted before nonces exist, so it cannot cover them. Editing a nonce in place breaks the hash chain immediately, but a ledger rebuilt from scratch around a different player nonce passes every check here until the final reveal, when the substituted entropy stops reproducing the published tails. Before then, only the genesis head you saved at session zero catches it. Genesis world and NPC nonces enter no derivation; they are a note for the record. A later activation's nonce is publicly declared and shapes that slot's columns.

What it cannot prove: omission is undetectable. The GM's machine can read every future value at any time, and reading ahead then declining to call for that check consumes nothing and logs nothing. Scheduling is likewise undetectable: arranging a pivotal scene around known-good queued values leaves no trace. And nothing forces the GM to log a draw. The only constraints on under-reporting are prompt publication and human memory, and at this volume memory is weak. The count guarantee is strong for ritual draws, where each is announced and every abandonment is a public void, and weak for routine ones: nobody remembers whether session 14 held nine or eleven secret Perception checks. Do not read one number as covering both.

The compensating control is statistical audit at reveal. Thousands of disclosed draws per player give the chi-square and runs tests real power; small samples would not. reveal-all, announce-before-draw, and ordered allocation are speed bumps and social ritual except where this verifier enforces them.

Later-activation beacon caveat: this offline file checks that the exact slot, lanes, nonce, and a future drand round were publicly declared before the randomness was used. It does not implement drand's BLS signature verification. Compare every activation's recorded chain, round, and randomness with an official drand explorer; without that, a GM-controlled beacon endpoint could invent randomness.
